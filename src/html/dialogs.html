    Help

    Beta tester guide

    Before you start

    • On an iPad or iPhone, open the link in Safari and add it to your Home Screen (Share › Add to Home Screen) before you set up your markers. The Home Screen app has its own storage: what you add in a Safari tab stays in the tab. Set up in a tab already? Back up there, then restore the file in the Home Screen app.
    • Back up once a week: Library › Back up. It’s a beta, and a backup file brings everything back.

    What it covers

    • Ohuhu and Copic markers only. Ohuhu: 364 markers, among them every colour in the Honolulu sets (24 up to the complete 320) and in the 48 Mid-tone, 104 Colors, 36 Skin Tones, 36 Gray Tones, 48 Pastel and 24 Portrait sets. Other Ohuhu markers with these codes work too. Copic: all 358 markers, by the codes Sketch, Ciao and Classic share.
    • Colours on screen are approximate: screens and inks differ. Markers › Print a swatch chart makes a page to colour in and compare.

    Pictures

    • Clear, closed lines work best: a downloaded colouring page, a scan, or a flat, evenly lit photo of a page (Photographing a page, below).
    • A page that came as a PDF: open it, screenshot the page, and choose the screenshot.

    Known limits

    • No account or sync: each device, and the Home Screen app, keeps its own data. Move it with Back up and Restore.
    • Very detailed, hatched or already-coloured pages, and faint or broken lines, give poor sections. ← Edit sections can merge, split or add them.
    • Picking one section on the picture needs touch or a mouse.

    What to try

    • Make a guide from a page you’d really colour. Try the Plan’s Pattern, Colours and Shading, then Colour along and tick sections off.
    • Print it or Save image, and try Reveal & share when you finish.

    How to report

    • Tap Send feedback (at the foot of Home, in the guide’s ⋯ menu, or in About, below). It adds your device and any recent errors; write what happened, and add a screenshot if you can.
    • When something goes wrong, Copy details beside the message copies what’s needed.
    Photographing a page
    • Lay the page flat on a table. Smooth out any curl.
    • Use even light: daylight near a window, or a bright room. Keep your shadow and your hand off the page, and avoid shiny glare.
    • Hold your phone or iPad above the page and get all of it in the frame. A bit of table around it is fine.
    • Taken at an angle? The app finds the page and straightens it. If it isn’t sure, drag the corners, then tap Straighten.
    • Clear, dark lines work best. If sections come out wrong, ← Edit sections lets you merge, split or add them.
    • Scans and screenshots work too. A colouring page that came as a PDF: open it, take a screenshot of the page, and choose the screenshot.
    Glossary
    Section
    One area of the picture, closed in by lines, that you colour with one marker. Its marker code is printed on it.
    Background / left out
    Parts that stay white. The background is the paper around the drawing. Left-out sections are ones you’ve tapped with Leave out in Edit sections; tap one there again to bring it back.
    Colour pattern
    How markers are spread over the page, in Pattern:
    • Gradient: colours flow across the page along a path (serpentine, vertical, diagonal or radial).
    • Random: markers scattered over the sections. Main colour gives one colour about 60% of the picture, a second 30% and an accent 10% (tap the bar to choose them); Mixed treats every marker alike, and No repeats gives each section its own.
    • Blend: tap to drop colour anchors; colours blend between them.
    • Photo: colours taken from a photo you lay over the page.
    • Manual: you pick every section’s marker.
    Flow
    The path a Gradient’s colours follow across the page (Pattern › Flow): Serpentine (in rows, back and forth), Vertical, Diagonal, Radial (rings out from a centre) or Around (round a centre, like a clock’s hand; good for mandalas).
    Look
    How a Gradient lays its markers along the flow (Pattern › Look): Smooth runs each colour gently into the next; Light to dark lays them in bands, light to dark within each; Auto chooses for the page and says which.
    Scatter
    How neatly a Gradient’s colours sit (Pattern › Scatter). Polished lays neat bands in flow order; Natural, Textured, Sparkle and Confetti mix them more and more, until it’s confetti close up and a rainbow from afar.
    Rough spots
    Touching sections of a Gradient whose colours jump. A line under the tabs counts them; Smooth them gives one of each pair a marker the guide isn’t using.
    Mood
    Which of your markers a palette or a Gradient draws on (Colours › Mood): Any, Bright, Soft, Pastel, Deep or Earthy.
    Include
    For a Gradient (Colours › Include): whether browns, greys and black, and fluorescents may be used. Each guide, and each zone, keeps its own choice.
    Surprise
    The ✨ button by the guide’s name: new colours and a new style in one tap (flow, mood, look, sometimes Random). Undo brings back what you had.
    Zone
    A part of the picture with its own pattern and colours, like a flower against the background around it. In Pattern, tap Zone, then tap or drag across sections to add them. The chips at the top of Pattern and Colours choose which zone those tabs change; Main is the rest of the picture. With shading on, each zone can be shaded or left flat, with its own roundness, highlights and shadows (Shading, under its chips); the light is the whole picture’s.
    Pin
    Keeps a section’s marker when you shuffle or change the pattern; a pinned section has a ring around its label. Tap a section, then Pin. Changing a section’s colour pins it too (Unpin lets the plan choose again).
    Kept
    A section you’ve coloured keeps its marker, whatever you change in the plan: it’s on your paper already. Kept sections have no ring; untick one and the plan can colour it again.
    Blend companions
    A lighter and a darker marker for each colour, listed in the printed key, for blending.
    Shading
    Adds light and shadow with lighter and darker markers you own. Drag the sun to move the light, or, with the Photo pattern, take the light from your photo (Light from).
    Highlight / Base / Shadow (H/B/S)
    The tones of a shaded section. B is its main marker. H is a lighter one on the side facing the light (or a warmer one, or the paper left white: Shading › Highlights), S a darker one on the far side (or a cooler or grey one laid over the base: Shading › Shadows). “2nd coat” means going over the base again.
    Greyscale
    The button with three grey bars under the picture (once called Values). It shows your guide in greys, so you can judge its values: where it’s light and dark, without the colours. It only changes the view.
    Colour along
    Where you colour your page: the markers are listed lightest first, and you tick off each section as you colour it. Your progress saves itself.
    Focus mode
    In Colour along. It zooms to one section at a time, colour by colour. Tap ✓ Done (or the section) to move on.
    Reveal
    In Share. Plays your page filling with colour, to share as a picture or a clip.
    Keyboard and screen readers
    • Picking a single section on the picture needs touch or a mouse: Change colour, Pin, Paint and ticking off one section.
    • By keyboard, use Colour along’s list instead: open a marker, then Mark all coloured or Find next. Focus mode and everything else work by keyboard too.
    • In Markers, press Shift+F10 on a marker to open its details.
    • In Change colour, Tab moves from one row or group of markers to the next, and the arrow keys within it.
    • A zone’s sections can be chosen by keyboard: in the zone’s editor, Tab to Choose sections with the keys. The arrow keys then move a ring from section to section, and Space adds the ringed one to the zone or takes it out.
    Your data
    • Your markers, palettes, guides and photos stay on this device. Nothing is uploaded.
    • There’s no account, no ads and no tracking. The app works offline.
    • On iPhone and iPad, Safari deletes a site’s data after about seven days without opening it. On your Home Screen (Share › Add to Home Screen) Marker Studio isn’t cleared like that, but it has its own storage: back up here first, then restore the file there.
    • Back up now and then: Library › Back up saves one file with everything. Library › Restore brings it back on any iPhone, iPad or computer.
    • A guide missing from your Library may still be stored on this device. Find lost guides looks for any and can add them back.

    Leaving the app, or starting again? Delete all my data removes your markers, palettes, guides and photos from this device.

    About

    Marker Studio

    Turns a photo of a colouring page into a marker-by-number guide, using the Ohuhu and Copic markers you own.

      Nothing you add leaves this device. There are no accounts, ads or analytics. It works offline, and the camera is used only when you Scan caps or Match a colour.

      How it works

      Step 1 of 3

      Photograph a colouring page

      Choose a photo of a line-art page, or try the sample.

      • Lay the page flat, in even light.
      • Get the whole page in the frame.
      • Taken at an angle? The app straightens it.
      Step 2 of 3

      Sections and colours

      The app finds the sections — the areas you’ll colour — and picks a marker for each, from the markers you own.

      • Tap a section to change its colour or pin it.
      • Try patterns and shading in the Pattern and Shading tabs.
      Step 3 of 3

      Colour along

      Tap Colour along and tick sections off as you colour them.

      • Focus mode zooms to one section at a time and walks you through.
      • Finished? Reveal & share shows your page filling with colour.
